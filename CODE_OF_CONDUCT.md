# Code of Conduct

This project uses the [Contributor Covenant, version 2.1](https://www.contributor-covenant.org/version/2/1/code_of_conduct/). This page is a short version of it, written for a small project. If the two ever disagree, the Covenant wins.

## What we want

Mossnote should be a place where anyone can take part without being made to feel unwelcome. That applies to issues, pull requests, reviews and any other space the project uses, and to anyone speaking for it in public.

Things that help:

- Be kind and patient, especially with people who are new.
- Disagree about the work, not the person.
- Give feedback you would be happy to receive, and take feedback without getting defensive.
- Own your mistakes, say sorry when it is called for, and fix them.
- Think about what is good for the project and its users as a whole.

## What is not acceptable

- Sexualised language or imagery, and unwelcome sexual attention.
- Insults, put-downs, trolling, and personal or political attacks.
- Harassment of any kind, in public or in private.
- Sharing someone's private information, such as an address or email, without their permission.
- Anything else a reasonable person would call inappropriate in a professional setting.

## Who this applies to

Everyone who takes part in the project: maintainers, contributors and people who just open an issue. It covers the repository and any space where someone represents the project.

## Reporting a problem

If you see or experience behaviour that breaks these rules, contact the maintainer. The contact details are on the profile of the repository owner, [@rahulkaushal04](https://github.com/rahulkaushal04). Please do not report it in a public issue.

Reports are read in confidence. The maintainer will look into each one, will not share who reported it, and will not hold a report against someone who made it in good faith.

## What happens next

The maintainer decides what to do. Depending on how serious it was, that can be:

1. **A private note.** A clear explanation of what was wrong and a request to stop.
2. **A warning.** A formal warning, with a period during which the person should not interact with the people involved.
3. **A temporary ban.** A set time away from the project, with no contact with the community.
4. **A permanent ban.** For a pattern of behaviour, or for harassment or serious abuse.
